1

Warm-up: which verb?

Use be for who, where or what something is like. Use have got for possessions and features.

BE

identity • age • description • place • feelings

I’m fifteen. She’s friendly.

My keys are in the bag.

HAVE GOT

possessions • family • features

I’ve got a bike.

The bag has got two pockets.

Be negatives: I’m not; he/she/it isn’t; you/we/they aren’t.

Be questions: Am I …? Is he …? Are they …?

Short answers: Yes, I am. / No, I’m not.   Yes, I have. / No, I haven’t.

Careful: She’s tall = she is tall. She’s got a bike = she has got a bike.

2

Story: The Lost Backpack

Read first for the story. Then read again and notice the verbs.

Word helper

locker
a small school cupboard with a door

pocket
a small bag part for carrying things

keys
you use them to open a door

gym
a room for sport

worried
not relaxed; thinking about a problem

behave got

Hi, I’m Leo. I’m fifteen, and I’m new at Brook School. My class isn’t big. I’ve got a new friend, Maya. She’s very funny, and she’s got a red backpack and a camera. We’ve got English on Monday mornings. Our teacher is Mr Green. He’s got a little dog, but it isn’t at school today.

Now I’m worried. My backpack isn’t in my locker. It’s grey, and it’s got two blue pockets. My English book and my keys are in it. My phone isn’t in it; it’s in my pocket.

“Is this your backpack?” asks Maya.
“No, it isn’t. That one is black.”
“Has your backpack got a name on it?” asks Mr Green.
“No, it hasn’t.”

Then we go to the gym. A grey backpack is on the floor.
“Are these your keys?” asks Mr Green.
“Yes, they are!”

Now I’m happy. My backpack isn’t lost, and we aren’t late for English.

Role-play

Round 1: Ask your teacher your six questions. Your teacher answers as Leo, Maya or Mr Green.

Round 2: Swap roles. Answer with Yes, he is. / No, it isn’t. / Yes, she has. / No, it hasn’t.

5

Speak and write about your world

Choose one topic: my bag, my room or my gadget.

1. Prepare a 30-second talk

My … is…It isn’t…It’s got…It hasn’t got…I’ve got…I haven’t got…
Speaking timer
0:30

2. Ask your teacher

Choose at least four questions. Then answer your teacher’s questions.

  • What colour is your bag/room/gadget?
  • Is it big or small? Is it old or new?
  • Where is it now?
  • What has it got?
  • What have you got in your bag?
  • What haven’t you got?
  • What is your favourite possession? Why?

3. Exit ticket · Write six sentences

Example: I’m sixteen. My bag isn’t new. I’ve got a tablet. I haven’t got a laptop. Where is my phone? Have you got a tablet?

Extra story: Max’s New Room

Use this now for more practice, or save it for your next lesson.

Word helper

bright = with a lot of light  •  shelves = plural of shelf  •  screen = the part of a laptop/phone that shows pictures  •  boxes = plural of box

Max is sixteen, and he’s got a new room. It’s small, but it’s bright. It’s got a bed, a desk and two shelves. Max hasn’t got a TV. His laptop is on the desk, and it’s got a large screen. His books aren’t on the shelves; they’re in boxes. His sister Mia is eight, and she’s got a lot of posters.

“Is your room ready?” she asks.
“No, it isn’t. Where are my headphones?”
“Are they in that box?”
“No, they aren’t.”
“Have you got them in your school bag?”
“No, I haven’t.”

Then Max looks under his bed. “Oh! They’re under my football!”

Read and answer

You can write short answers.

Answer: He’s sixteen.
Answer: It’s got a bed, a desk and two shelves.
Answer: No, he hasn’t.
Answer: They’re in boxes.
Answer: His sister Mia has.
Answer: They’re under his football.

Make it personal

Write four sentences about your room. Use is/isn’t, are/aren’t, has got/hasn’t got and have got/haven’t got.
